# Accuracy diagnosis: where the flood errors are

Generated by `eval/diagnose_errors.py` on 2026-10-13 from leave-one-event-out run `20261012T165259Z` of `eval/results/flood.csv`, at the boundary tolerance the gates file declares (1 px = 20 m).

**This document changes nothing.** No pipeline code and no parameter was touched to produce it. The case study was not used (Rule 4).

**Where each number comes from (Rule 3.8).** Two kinds appear here and they must not be confused. The ACCURACY numbers - `F1 tol`, `F1`, and every control false-flood fraction - are the rows of `eval/results/flood.csv` for the run named above, and nothing here recomputes or adjusts them. Everything else is a DIAGNOSTIC COUNT: pixel tallies, position shares, boundary distances, shift scans and oracle bounds, computed here from each event's own score file under `runs/eval/<event>/scores/` and from the cached rasters that score was produced from, using the metric definitions in `validation/compare.py`. Diagnostic counts are not accuracy claims and must never be quoted as accuracy; they say where the error in those measured scores is. Every one of them is recorded in `docs/accuracy_diagnosis.json` beside this file, which carries the `run_id` and lets the document be re-rendered without re-running anything (`--from-json`).

Each event was re-run with the configuration ITS leave-one-event-out fold was scored with, read from that event's own score file, and the error map taken apart. The intermediate masks come from a replica of `strandline.detect.detect_flood` whose rebuilt class map is asserted byte-identical to the real one; an event whose replica disagreed would be reported as an error here rather than attributed.

## What these numbers name

### The single largest FIXABLE miss source in the steep events

**The conjunctive magnitude gate** - `rough_water` requires BOTH polarisations to move by at least `min_abs_change_db` (3 dB), in the same direction, before a pixel counts as changed at all. It accounts for 35.0% of every missed reference pixel in the steep events (2247 px), and 1267 of those - 19.8% of all steep-event misses - are pixels where ONE polarisation moved 3 dB or more and the other did not. A further 827 px failed because neither channel reached 3 dB. Sign disagreement is negligible (2 px), so it is the magnitude conjunction doing the damage, not the agreement requirement.

It is the largest *fixable* source rather than the largest source. The bigger bucket - 37.0% of steep-event misses, with no seed-strength z-score in either polarisation - is almost entirely **changeless ground**: of those pixels only 115 carry a coherent change large enough to join a region at all, against 2256 that carry no qualifying change in either polarisation. No threshold, seed or growth rule recovers a pixel whose backscatter did not move; those are a sensor-and-scale limit and must be reported as one. The magnitude gate, by contrast, discards pixels the detector demonstrably saw.

The next source down is the drainage-connection test at 13.3% of steep-event misses, which matters and is addressed by the second fix below.

### The false-alarm source on the controls

**Flat valley-floor ground away from the channel**, admitted by the either-sign rule: 52.1% of all 10692 false-flood pixels pooled over 4 controls, against 25.7% in the channel corridor and 19.5% on a permanent-water shore. 6184 of the pooled pixels BRIGHTENED and 4508 darkened.

The worst control, `ecuador_poza_honda_control_2024`, carries 7780 of them - 0.0164 of its analysed ground against the declared 1% ceiling - and **the reservoir-shore hypothesis for it is now tested and false**: its distance histogram above puts only a small minority within 3 px of permanent water and the bulk of the pixels 10 px (200 m) or further from any water the detector found. They sit on low-HAND, gentle-slope valley floor in a dry August, they are radar-only (optical refused that date for cloud), and they are brightening-dominated. A coherent 3 dB brightening of flat ground in a tropical farmed valley is what soil-moisture change, irrigation and tillage look like to C-band; `rough_water` calls it water because inside the valley-floor zone it tests the MAGNITUDE of a change and not its sign, and asks nothing about whether the post-event surface could physically hold water.

### Two things these errors are NOT

**Not a geometric offset.** The +-5 px cross-correlation finds its best shift at or within two pixels of zero on every event, the directions are inconsistent between events (`bhutan_punakha_2024` (-2, -2) for +0.0204; `bosnia_konjic_2024` (+1, +3) for +0.0182; `ecuador_poza_honda_2025` (+0, +1) for +0.0392; `norway_ringebu_2023` (+0, -1) for +0.0217; `norway_selsverket_2023` (+0, +0) for +0.0000; `pakistan_larkana_2022` (+0, +0) for +0.0000), and the largest gain any shift buys is small. A single registration error would show one consistent direction and a large gain. DIAGNOSIS ONLY: no shift is applied anywhere, and fitting one against the reference would be fitting to the reference.

**Not an edge-of-polygon disagreement.** In the steep events only 4.1% (`bhutan_punakha_2024`), 2.7% (`bosnia_konjic_2024`), 18.6% (`ecuador_poza_honda_2025`), 17.5% (`norway_selsverket_2023`) of errors sit within 1 px of a correct detection, so the steep-event failure is whole missing reaches and not a one-pixel rim. The measured median channel width there is 2 px (`bhutan_punakha_2024`), 7.21 px (`bosnia_konjic_2024`), 4.47 px (`ecuador_poza_honda_2025`), 2.83 px (`norway_selsverket_2023`) at 20 m, which is the scale the mixed-pixel argument rests on.

### The physics-based fix for each

Neither is implemented here, and neither is a retune: both change a test, and a test change needs its own declared decision, its own leave-one-event-out pass and its own control check before anything is adopted.

**1. For the misses: a per-polarisation, noise-aware change test instead of one shared dB threshold demanded of both channels.** Note first what the split says: VV is the channel that falls short on 641 of those px and VH on 626, so this is NOT a VH-is-noisy story - it is a single threshold being wrong for both channels. The physics is the sensor's own noise budget. Sentinel-1 IW GRDH has a noise-equivalent sigma-zero of about -22 dB, each polarisation sits a different distance above it (and over a smooth or partly-water surface VH sits lower in absolute terms than VV), and speckle adds a per-channel uncertainty set by the equivalent number of looks (`enl` 4.4, reduced by the Lee filter). Whichever channel comes closest to its floor gives a *clipped* response to a real water change, so demanding the same 3 dB from both discards the pixel - the 1267 px measured above, in whichever direction the clipping happened to fall. The fix is to compare each polarisation's change with that polarisation's OWN uncertainty, derived from the published NESZ and from the pre-event stack's own ENL-corrected variance, to require the change only in the channels that are above their noise floor, and to keep the polarisation requirement as a SIGN-consistency test where both channels are usable - which the measured 2 px of sign disagreement says costs almost nothing. Every quantity in it comes from the sensor specification and from the scene's own pre-event statistics, so it is one rule for every area and date, not a value fitted to a gorge.

**2. For the control false alarms: require a water surface that hydraulics allows, not merely a change on low ground.** Water fills a valley from the channel upward along the drainage network and stands at a common surface within a reach; wet soil, irrigated fields and tilled ground do not. Two constraints follow, both from terrain layers the pipeline already computes (`flow_dir`, `accumulation`, HAND) and both applicable everywhere. (a) Replace the single global HAND ceiling with a *reach-relative* one: a candidate pixel must lie below the water surface implied by the channel reach it actually drains to, rather than below a fixed 20 m above the nearest drainage anywhere in the scene. (b) Replace component-level drainage connection with per-pixel flow-path continuity along the D8 directions: today a whole connected component is admitted if any ONE of its pixels touches a channel within 3 px, which is how a field-sized patch enters, and the same coarse test is costing 13.3% of steep-event misses in the other direction by rejecting genuine flood that reaches the channel only outside the 3 px buffer. A continuity test tightens the controls and loosens the gorges with one change, because both failures come from the same component-level approximation.
